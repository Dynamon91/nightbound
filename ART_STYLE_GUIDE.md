# Nightbound — Mockup-Fidelity Art Direction

This guide is the working target for replacing prototype geometry with authored-looking 3D assets. It is an original visual direction and does not copy Spyro assets.

## Hero — Nightbound Bat

- Keep the silhouette readable at gameplay distance: large expressive head, compact torso, broad wing membranes, distinct ears and glowing eyes.
- Replace primitive spheres with a low-poly sculpted mesh using controlled bevels and asymmetrical organic forms.
- Use three material families: dark violet body, slightly lighter wing membrane, luminous eye accents.
- Add visible membrane ribs and a small number of silhouette-defining tufts rather than noisy micro-detail.
- Animation targets: idle breathing, anticipatory crouch, jump, glide, bank-left/right, landing, burst, damage and celebration.

## Moonlit Valley

- Ground should read as layered stylized terrain, not a collection of cubes.
- Primary forms: rounded grassy plateaus, exposed rock shelves, broken ledges, shallow ravines and elevated glide routes.
- Rocks should use 5–7-sided silhouettes with a large primary plane and two or three secondary facets.
- Vegetation should be clustered in families with deliberate empty spaces around gameplay routes.
- Use cool moonlight with restrained warm accents around collectibles and landmarks.
- Background silhouettes should establish depth and navigation without becoming gameplay obstacles.

## The Veilwood

- Establish a clearly different silhouette language from Moonlit Valley: taller trunks, denser vertical framing, darker violet foliage and bioluminescent accents.
- Build layered forest depth with foreground trunks, midground platforms and distant canopy silhouettes.
- Use small luminous plants and spores as navigation accents rather than covering every surface in glow.
- Keep traversal lanes readable through controlled contrast and negative space.

## Collectibles and landmarks

- Crystals: cyan, angular, bright but compact; readable from a distance.
- Moonshards: pink-violet, rarer, positioned on risky aerial detours.
- Moonseeds: softer magical shapes with a distinct silhouette from crystals.
- Checkpoints: luminous rings with a clear activation state and enough contrast to remain visible in fog.
- Major landmarks should be visible from multiple traversal routes and use one dominant silhouette rather than many tiny props.

## Camera and presentation

- Preserve a third-person camera with enough vertical framing to show the hero, immediate landing target and the next aerial route.
- Favor depth through layered silhouettes, fog and lighting rather than excessive post-processing.
- Avoid generic repeated props: rotate, scale and cluster reusable assets intentionally.

## Quality gate before calling the mockup target reached

1. Hero silhouette is recognizable without relying on HUD labels.
2. Main platforms have authored silhouettes instead of primitive-box outlines.
3. Each realm has a visibly different material and vegetation language.
4. Collectibles, checkpoints and landmarks remain readable during motion.
5. A representative screenshot contains foreground, playable midground and distant background layers.
6. Lighting supports navigation and mood without hiding geometry.
7. Music and visual identity agree: Moonlit Valley is lively and nocturnal; Veilwood is mysterious and suspended; bosses become denser and more aggressive.
